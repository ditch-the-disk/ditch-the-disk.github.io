Diagnostic imaging MVP requirements

Proposed for workgroup and CMS review · November 20, 2026

The aim of the imaging MVP is for patients and care teams to access complete, full-fidelity imaging studies through apps and other software clients of their choice.

Proposed requirements by role

Each participant implements the roles it performs. The MVP covers retrieval from a known source through the SMART Imaging Access API. As CMS-aligned networks make clinical records available, the same imaging API can serve requests authorized through those networks.

Providers

Requirement Description
Images and reports Make full-fidelity images available through the imaging server and reports through the clinical system. Where possible, include the Study Instance UID or accession number in the report so clients can match the two.
Outside studies Give care teams a routine way to retrieve prior scans before a visit and to safely view and use studies patients share.
Production access Set a target date for making images available to patients and care teams.

Authorization servers

Requirement Description
Patient access Let patients grant an app of their choice access to their imaging studies through their existing provider.
Clinician access Systems that launch clinician apps within the organization should also support imaging access for those apps.
Automated treatment requests (optional, encouraged) Authorize pre-approved outside providers and referral services to retrieve permitted studies for treatment.
Permission checks Let the imaging server validate the token and confirm the requester, patient, granted permissions, and any study restrictions the authorization server manages.

Imaging servers and archives

Imaging networks, PACS, vendor-neutral archives, cloud services, and EHR imaging modules can serve as imaging servers. A separate archive can supply studies to the imaging server over DICOMweb.

Requirement Description
Service discovery Publish the imaging and authorization service information through SMART discovery. When a separate clinical FHIR endpoint is the entry point, identify the imaging server there.
Study search and retrieval List permitted studies through FHIR ImagingStudy. Deliver complete studies, individual series, or individual instances through DICOMweb at the fidelity retained by the archive.
Access controls Validate the access token on every request and apply both the granted permissions and local access rules. Support patient-chosen apps without requiring a separate business agreement for each app.

Apps and other clients

Requirement Description
Study retrieval Discover the servers or use a configured address, obtain authorization, find a permitted study, and download it in full.
Use of images Patient apps let patients view and share their images. Treatment uses include reviewing a study during a visit and retrieving prior scans for a referral.
Delayed or refused downloads Retry after the requested delay. If the server refuses a download, search again for the studies available to the app.

Testing and deployment

Every participant hosts or joins at least one public sandbox and takes part in a demonstration there. A sandbox is a shared test environment where teams connect their systems using synthetic patients and realistic imaging studies.

  1. Prepare the servers. Authorization and imaging server teams test their endpoints and make them available to other participants. Imaging servers supply sample CT, MRI, and X-ray studies.
  2. January 2027 milestone. Developers of apps and other clients connect to another team’s server in a sandbox and demonstrate retrieval of a complete study using patient authorization or a pre-authorized treatment request.
  3. Test at provider sites. Server and client teams deploy the software in each participating provider’s test environment. Care teams test the workflow, and the provider verifies that its access rules are enforced.

For protocol details and testing resources, see the workgroup MVP and testing plan, SMART Imaging Access implementation guide, and reference sandbox.
