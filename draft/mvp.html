Ditch the Disk: proposed MVP and pledge

A proposal for what organizations in the Ditch the Disk workgroup would pledge to build this fall, and how they would test it together.

Background

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) runs the Health Tech Ecosystem, a voluntary pledge program. In July 2026 CMS launched its Diagnostic Imaging workgroup, known as Ditch the Disk, with an initial draft pledge: full-fidelity images, meaning the original image files, for patient access, treatment, and payment. The workgroup is writing the actual pledge, with enough detail to build and test, by November 1.

Patients can already connect an app to their hospital and download their clinical records, because certified EHRs must offer a standard API. Images live in separate imaging systems that these apps cannot reach, so clinicians who need outside images often wait for a CD or request them by phone and fax. In a kickoff survey of 22 participants, most said patients still get images on CDs, though most of their health systems already run standard APIs for clinical data.

SMART Imaging Access is a specification for putting images behind the sign-in that apps already use. It combines SMART on FHIR for sign-in and permission, FHIR for health data, and DICOMweb for medical images. An app that a patient or clinician has approved uses the same permission to list the patient's imaging studies and download the images. The current draft is under review at HL7, the standards organization that publishes FHIR.

Proposed pledge

We propose a minimum viable product, or MVP: the smallest set of capabilities that lets patients and clinicians get images without a disk. The pledge below says what each kind of organization commits to, and would replace the initial draft that CMS published at launch. The sections after it turn those commitments into roles and requirements that can be tested.

We pledge to make diagnostic images available electronically for patient access, treatment, and other permitted uses, so that sharing images with any provider is as easy as handing over a disc.

We will use standards-based APIs (for example, FHIR and DICOMweb as profiled in SMART Imaging Access). Patients will reach their images with the same sign-in they already use for their clinical records, and with CMS-aligned digital identity credentials as networks adopt them. This lets any approved app get the images, and it lays groundwork for network-based exchange.

We will start testing with other participants by January 2027, using the workgroup's MVP as the common path, and use what we learn to improve it and drive real-world adoption.

We will meet the commitments below that apply to us:

  • Providers make full-fidelity images available to any app a patient chooses and to other providers for treatment, first in a test environment and then for real patients. They accept images that patients share electronically instead of asking for a disc: open shared links, view the images, and download the DICOM files for use in care. They do the security review and setup needed to make this routine.
  • Companies that handle sign-in for providers, such as EHR and identity vendors, let authorized users approve apps for imaging access, tell apps where to find the images, and confirm to imaging systems what each app is allowed to see.
  • Companies that store or serve images, such as PACS, archive and cloud vendors, deliver full-fidelity studies through standards-based APIs that work with the provider's sign-in, without a separate business agreement for each app.
  • App and service developers retrieve full-fidelity studies through these APIs for patients or clinicians. Patient apps will let patients re-share what they retrieve, for example with a time-limited, revocable link or QR code that gives the recipient a viewer and a full-fidelity download.

Principles

One imaging server for all requestersPatients, clinicians, and organizations use the same study search and image download. Only the way permission is granted differs.
Existing standardsSMART, FHIR, and DICOMweb are used as they are deployed today, with no new intermediary that everyone must pay for.
Original imagesClients receive the original DICOM files.
A building block for networksIt starts with one hospital or imaging center offering images directly, and grows as health information networks connect to the same interface.
Local access rulesEach organization's own systems decide who may see which studies. The MVP enforces those decisions without defining them.

Requirements by role

Each commitment in the pledge maps to one or more roles. Providers fill the provider role. Companies that handle sign-in fill the authorization server role. Companies that store or serve images fill the imaging server role, with the image archive behind or within it. App and service developers fill the client role.

Technical details for each role are in the SMART Imaging Access specification.

Provider

A health system, hospital, imaging center, or practice.

  • Set up a test environment with synthetic patients and realistic studies, including at least a CT, an MRI, and a plain X-ray.
  • Make reports available through the clinical system and images through the imaging server. Including each study's unique ID or accession number in its report is recommended, so apps can match reports to images.
  • Set a date for making images available to real patients.
  • Accept images that patients share electronically instead of asking for a disc, and do the security review and setup needed to make this routine.

Authorization server

Often part of an EHR, or a separate identity or access service.

  • Let patients and clinicians approve apps for imaging with the same sign-in they use for clinical data.
  • Publish the imaging server's location in the configuration information the server already provides to apps.
  • Confirm to the imaging server what each client is allowed to see.
  • Access for pre-approved systems that run with no user present, such as a referral service, is optional and encouraged. A server that supports only this kind of access does not meet the MVP.

Imaging server

A service in front of an image archive, an archive with these interfaces built in, a cloud imaging platform, or an EHR's imaging module.

  • List a patient's studies using FHIR.
  • Return the original DICOM files for a whole study, a series, or a single image.
  • Small preview images are recommended, so apps can show thumbnails before downloading a study.
  • Check the permission on every request, and release only the studies the provider's access rules allow. The specification describes ways to divide this work between an EHR, an imaging server, and an image archive.

Image archive

A PACS, vendor-neutral archive, or cloud archive that supplies an imaging server instead of serving apps itself. In the pledge, this is part of the imaging server role.

  • Let the imaging server search for studies and retrieve the original DICOM files through DICOMweb.
  • The imaging server and the archive arrange their own connection and credentials. Apps never connect to a separate archive directly.

Client

A patient's app, a clinician's viewer, a clinical intake tool, or a referral service.

  • Find the imaging server from the authorization server's configuration, or use a configured address.
  • Download a full study, then display it, import it, or give it to the patient.
  • Let patients re-share what they retrieve, for example with a link or QR code.
  • Wait and retry when the imaging server asks, and search again if a download is refused.

Outside the MVP: finding which organizations hold a patient's records, matching patients across organizations, and trust between organizations, which other Health Tech Ecosystem workgroups are addressing. Also outside it: importing studies into another organization's PACS, and other search methods such as DICOM's QIDO-RS.

Testing

Testing the software does not require a health system. A basic test needs one client, one authorization server, and one imaging server, running on synthetic patients and studies in a vendor's sandbox. The specification's open-source reference software runs a public test server at imaging.argo.run that can stand in for any of these roles, and it will be updated before testing starts.

Testing fits into this timeline:

  • November 1Pledge agreed, and participants named for each role.
  • November and DecemberVendors bring their parts online in their own sandboxes and test against the public test server.
  • January 2027Vendors test against each other's sandboxes in the scenarios below, including at a virtual connectathon.
  • Early 2027Health systems set up tested scenarios in their own test environments, with their own configuration and access rules, then make images available to real patients.
  • May 2027HL7 consensus vote on SMART Imaging Access, including changes from testing.

The first round needs two or three each of authorization servers, imaging servers, and image archives, and three or four clients. Three to five health systems would follow as providers.

Testing succeeds when a patient's app downloads a full study from a pledged health system after one sign-in, and a referral service at another organization downloads a permitted study without a CD or a phone call.

The specification's reference software plays every role on synthetic data. A vendor replaces one part with its own and tests it against the rest.

The EHR handles sign-in. Either the EHR or the PACS vendor provides the imaging server, and the PACS supplies the images.

An imaging center with no EHR uses a standalone authorization service, such as an access-management company or a records-request company it already works with. The imaging server is built into its PACS, or runs as open-source software in front of it. A regional health information exchange could run these for several centers.

Glossary

20 terms
Access token
A temporary credential an app receives after the user approves it. The app presents it with each request.
Accession number
An ID the radiology department assigns to an imaging order. It is unique within one organization.
DICOM
The international standard for medical images and their details. Nearly every imaging device and archive uses it.
DICOMweb
DICOM's web API. WADO-RS is the part for downloading images, and QIDO-RS is the part for searching.
EHR
Electronic health record system. Certified EHRs must offer standard APIs for clinical data.
FHIR
Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources, HL7's web API standard for health data.
Full fidelity
The original DICOM image files, with no compression loss and all their details.
Health information exchange
An organization that moves health data among providers in a region or network.
HL7
Health Level Seven International, the standards organization that publishes FHIR.
IHE
Integrating the Healthcare Enterprise, a group that writes profiles combining existing standards. XCA-I is its profile for sharing images across communities.
Image archive
A system that stores DICOM studies, such as a PACS or VNA. In the MVP it supplies studies to an imaging server.
Imaging server
The system that lists a patient's studies and delivers images to approved apps, checking permission on every request.
ImagingStudy
The FHIR resource that describes one imaging study and where to download its images.
PACS and VNA
Picture archiving and communication system, and vendor-neutral archive. Systems that store images.
SMART App Launch
The SMART on FHIR process in which a patient or clinician signs in and approves an app.
SMART Backend Services
The SMART on FHIR process for pre-approved systems that run with no user present.
SMART Imaging Access
The specification that extends SMART on FHIR permission to imaging studies and images.
Study
One imaging exam, such as a chest CT. A study contains series, and a series contains individual images.
Study Instance UID
The globally unique ID that DICOM assigns to every study.
Token introspection
A standard way for a service to ask the authorization server whether a token is valid and what it allows.
